# v50.1 readiness-review notes (started 8 Nov 2026)

Items the architect has sent to the readiness review. Add to this list; do not close an item without a recorded ruling.

1. **Rollback v50 -> v49 during a boot storm (residual; architect, 8 Nov 2026).** The chip's external watchdog stays armed across a flash. v49's boot-storm holdoff sleep (six early resets, a 600 s ULP sleep) runs before v49's first watchdog service: in `daec561` the storm sleep is at `Generalized-Core-Counter.cpp:~396-405` and the vendored `setWDT` at `:811`, the first loop service at `~:1176` (`lib/AB1805_RK/AB1805_RK.cpp:65-70`). If a v49 image boots into a retained boot-storm state while the chip dog is still armed, the dog can reset the board within 124 s and cut the holdoff short. This is **pre-existing in v49's structure** (found by Codex in the integrated Stage 7 round 1, Finding 2 rollback paragraph), not introduced by v50, and **not a bench case**: bench item 2 covers the normal rollback only. v50.1 itself closes the same hole on its own boot path (round-1 Finding 1: the external watchdog is initialised right after the bind and stopped/started around the storm sleep).
2. **The stale evidence sentinel (accepted residual; architect, 8 Nov 2026).** A stale 0xA5 can mislabel at most one later reason-20 classification as a power-up wake (a flush that fails, a reset between the write and the clear, or a rollback through v49, which ignores the byte); it is cleared at the next completed v50 boot. A complete fix would need a v49.1 change; out of scope.
3. **The I21 continuity decision** goes to Grok explicitly at the readiness review (`docs/reviews/2026-11-grok-progress/triage.md`).
4. **The boot-storm detector counts only reset reasons 0 and 60 in the real build (found 9 Nov; pre-existing, v49.1 and earlier; needs a ruling).** The switch at `Generalized-Core-Counter.cpp:338-352` lists `NONE`, `PANIC`, `WATCHDOG`, `UNKNOWN` and `USER_APPLICATION`, but `PANIC`, `UNKNOWN` and `USER_APPLICATION` sit behind `#ifdef`s on names that are enumerators in Device OS 6.6.0, not macros, so they are compiled out. Verified by a Device OS 6.6.0 build of the integration head (`1d1d5af`) with `#pragma message` probes at the switch: all six names (`NONE`, `PANIC`, `WATCHDOG`, `UNKNOWN`, `USER_APPLICATION`, `PIN_RESET`) printed `NOT_A_MACRO` (make rc 0). Consequence: a panic loop (reason 130) or an unknown-reason loop (10) never trips the storm protection; `USER_APPLICATION` does not exist in 6.6.0. The structural test `retained_boot_structural_test.py` passes because it checks only the source text (it asserts the `case RESET_REASON_PANIC:` text), not what the build compiles. Candidate fix (a small WO, test-first): direct `case RESET_REASON_PANIC:` and `case RESET_REASON_UNKNOWN:` labels (both are enumerators in 6.6.0), drop `USER_APPLICATION`, and a compile-level test; whether to count `UNKNOWN` is an architect call. Not benched with 5001; the early-reset image (5096) benches the storm path with reason 60.
5. **S1b has no retry on a bound chip (found 15-16 Nov in the 3a investigation; does not block v50.1; architect, 16 Nov).** S1b runs only on an unbound boot (`Generalized-Core-Counter.cpp:294` at `1d1d5af`). If its chip access fails on that boot, the code logs `PreBindRtc: chip access failed` and continues to `ensureBound` (`:295`), which binds. The next boot is bound, S1b never runs again, and a unit with old-library history keeps WRTC = 0, so Device OS's settime silently no-ops. Reading of the code, not observed on a unit. Candidate fixes, the cheaper one wins (at most about 5 `src/` lines): (a) when S1b fails on an unbound boot, do not bind on that boot, so the next boot retries; or (b) read WRTC on bound boots and raise a visible flag. Scoped into the v50.2 L WO (`WO-2026-11-08-003`, "The change" item 5). A fleet unit upgrading from 4901 starts unbound (v49 deletes any binding), so only the chip-access-failure case is exposed.
6. **The chip-watchdog feed (found 15-16 Nov; fixed by WO-2026-11-16-001, PR #237 merged 17 Nov; architect).** On 5001/1d1d5af the fleet config passed `.watchdogPin(D5)`, so `feedWatchdog()` only toggled the GPIO and the AM1805 reset the device about 124 s after every wake (Dev-09 #52-#54, then every hourly wake on 16 Nov; Dev-14 control test, `connectionMode: 0`). The fix removes the pin so the feed writes the WDT register, as the legacy firmware did; a failed refresh is logged when its return code changes. **Readiness notes:** (a) D5's wiring to the chip's WDI pin is **unverified**; the fleet now feeds through the register. (b) The 124 s reset reason was **confirmed by timing, not by a captured reason**. (c) 3a passed (16 Nov rerun). (d) The fix is **accepted on the bench (17 Nov)**: fixed 5001 on Dev-14 ran about 12 min and then about 25 min with no reset, and 5097 on the fixed base still resets every 124 s (5 resets, 03:24:55Z to 03:33:11Z); item 5 needs two nights on Dev-09 or Dev-14 (17 and 18 Nov), still to run. (e) **Side effect of the old build (architect, 17 Nov; a suspicion, not proven):** the 124 s reset loop also restarted the modem attach on every reset, so Dev-09 made repeated cellular attach attempts all day on 16 Nov, which can cause carrier or network back-off. Dev-09 has not registered since (`ConnSummary: fail last=CELLULAR_ACQUIRE sig=na` at 04:29Z and 05:25Z on 17 Nov, while Dev-14 beside it connects in under 15 s on the same build). Dev-09 was powered off (USB and battery) from about 09:19Z on 17 Nov. **Observed (18 Nov): recovered after about 22 h off.** It was powered on at about 06:56:50Z; the first attempt still failed (`ConnSummary: fail last=CELLULAR_ACQUIRE sig=na`, 660 s, 07:07:54Z), and at the next wake (about 07:25:46Z) it registered in 87 s and reached the cloud at about 07:29:33Z on v5001, then drained its queued 16 Nov records. This fits the back-off suspicion; it does not prove it. The soak binary is the CI build of `eb398b8` (run 43987729413, SHA-256 `ffa84c20...89ec`); if v50.1 is tagged from that head, it is the release binary.
