**1. Verdict**

The project is on a sound path to a field release candidate. The cores that previously concentrated risk have been partially decomposed, clock ownership is named, flush-before-reset exists for deliberate paths, the recovery ladder is explicit, and the release pipeline has real gates. The single biggest risk is still data trust for occupancy: clock trust does not survive a restart, untrusted sessions previously produced wrong credited spans (fixed only after the v49.1 tag), and the planned v50 AB1805 switch can re-introduce dual ownership or an armed chip watchdog across rollback. Those paths can produce wrong counts or a device that is dark and unrecoverable by remote means. The pack is unusually honest about this; the remaining work is to close the ownership and persistence gaps before RC1 rather than to keep adding layers.

**3. Answers to the pack’s eight questions**

1. A persisted last-sync epoch is sound only if it is withdrawn on a large correction, an invalid RTC read, or an oscillator-fault indication. The current “this boot only” rule is safer than a sticky trust bit, but it forces every post-reset session into the untrusted path. The failure mode the rule hides is a device whose RTC is slowly drifting or was set wrong once and then never corrected because trust never returns.

2. Accepting the loss of at most one partial session per reboot while untrusted is the right trade for people-counting. Crediting a merged span (the previous bug) is worse than a bounded under-count. The 49.7-day `millis()` alias is acceptable if the product never stays continuously awake that long without a trusted sync.

3. The deliberate-reset flush covers the seven sites. The paths that still lose state are exactly those listed: OOM, the two hardware watchdogs, the ApplicationWatchdog callback, and HIBERNATE. No additional store appears to have been missed; the risk is the ones already excluded by design.

4. The switch plan is safe only if the three pre-ship bench cases (binding present, rollback with chip WDT armed, wake reason after native POWER_OFF) pass and v49 remains the rollback floor. The residual risk is an armed chip watchdog that survives the unbind, or a window in which both the vendored library and Device OS claim the chip. The spike’s P7 result shows the vendored `deepPowerDown` works; that does not prove the native path is equivalent under rollback.

5. The ladder is sound for unattended devices. Unknown=open prevents the circular “wedged radio prevents trust, trust required for recovery” case. Stage 3 is a last resort that can leave a device dark; that is the correct trade when no remote reset exists. Cooldowns and device-ID jitter are adequate. The low-battery hard-stage suppression is correct.

6. The pipeline is adequate for a public repo. The version check, pinned actions, environment-gated upload, and rulesets are real controls. The one-time secret scan is better than nothing; a scheduled scan would be better. No workflow can pin or flash, which is the important property.

7. The process fails silently where all three AIs share the same incomplete model of Device OS or AB1805 behavior that only appears after a real hibernate or a chip-watchdog event. The verifier quoting the SHA is good; it does not catch a shared assumption that “the native driver behaves like the vendored library.” The human gate is the only control that can catch that class of error.

8. Cut the residual PMIC forensic retained fields still declared from `SensorManager.h`, the unused diagnostic publish path when it is not the product default, and any remaining one-off test hooks that are not behind an explicit bench profile. The smallest safe core is the state machine, the named clock/trust owner, the flush-before-reset path, the connectivity failsafe, and the persistence facade that already has versioned layouts.

9. Genuine improvement: the two largest cores shrank, stubs are gone, a single owner of “what time is it and can it be trusted” exists, and deliberate resets flush. Moved complexity: total `src/` grew, the persistence fan-in relocated to `SystemConfig.h`, and the time module is now 2 499 lines. The structural ownership work is real; the net simplification is not yet visible in the tree size.

**4. What to cut**

- The six `extern retained` PMIC anomaly fields still declared in `SensorManager.h` (and defined in `power/`). Reason: domain leakage the September review already flagged; the policy already moved. Risk of removal: loss of those specific counters; low if the power module owns the equivalent telemetry.
- The vendored `AB1805_RK` after the native-driver switch is proven (v50.2). Reason: dual ownership risk. Risk of removal: none once the native path and rollback floor are verified.
- Most of the per-category trace flags and the diagnostics-publish batch when they are not the release default. Reason: additive tissue. Risk of removal: harder field diagnosis on the next novel failure; acceptable if the remaining breadcrumbs and status payload are kept.
