## 1. Context (one page)
- **What GCC does.** Firmware for Particle Boron (nRF52840, Device OS **6.6.0**) people-counting and occupancy sensors in outdoor and indoor sites (PIR; the framework supports other sensors). Low-power by design: the device sleeps almost all the time (ULP/STOP sleep through the day, a nightly **hibernate** after the close), wakes on a PIR or a timer, connects over cellular only when it must, and reports to a cloud webhook (Ubidots) and Particle ledgers. Hourly or event-driven reports; a daily close at the site's closing hour; configuration arrives from Particle ledgers. Eastern-time sites; one product, **42131**.
- **The fleet.** 13 units in product 42131: sites in North Carolina and Ontario (court/pickleball/tennis counters, a trail counter on solar, church and lab occupancy pilots) plus two **bench devices in Singapore** (Dev-09, Dev-14) on a poor Singtel connection that are the soak devices and deliberately stress connectivity.
- **Release history, v44 to v49.1** (`CHANGELOG.md`): v44 Device OS 6.4.1 → 6.6.0 (application unchanged); v45 *PowerSplit* and v46 *SleepSplit* (behaviour-neutral refactors of the power and sleep code, "Step 6"); v47 *PersistFreeze* (two small behaviour changes); v48 *OccupancyEndsAtClose* (a session open at the close is credited to the close; nothing between close and opening counts); **v49.1 *DurableResets*** (Eastern default time zone, flush-before-reset at seven sites, USB charge-latch recovery, a guard that deletes a stored Device OS RTC binding). Each release was soaked on the two bench devices before Chip set it as the product default.
- **Roles** (`AI_DEVELOPMENT_WORKFLOW.md` §2-§4): an **architect** (an AI chat) rules on design; **Claude Code** is the workflow controller (dispatches, records, runs read-only checks and the CI/release steps); **GitHub Copilot** implements (Stage 6); **Codex** verifies independently (Stage 7, read-only plus local builds; a verdict must quote the exact commit SHA under review); **Chip** is the engineer and final authority, and the **only one who merges, pins firmware or sets a product default**. Every change has a Work Order with a plain-language goal, a source-line budget and stop rules; at most two verification rounds before an architect ruling.

## 4. Known limitations and open items
Open recovery-plan items (`docs/RECOVERY_PLAN_2026-09-26.md`):
- **19** untrusted-clock sessions merge and are credited as one span (Dev-09: 205 min credited for about 97 real). Fix designed and verified, **held for v50**: C1 (`debounceRemainingMs()` on `millis()`; PR #185) and C2 (credit each untrusted piece by measured on-time; PR #184). **Accepted bounded loss:** at most one partial session lost per reboot while untrusted; a piece open 49.7 days aliases the 32-bit `millis()`.
- **21** clock trust should survive a restart and be withdrawn on evidence (v50, after the native-driver switch).
- **23** a stale fuel gauge (SoC stayed low while the cell read full); **24** power and operating-schedule consolidation; **26** the single safe-reset owner (v49 shipped the durability half: the flush at seven sites; the one-owner redesign follows the AB1805 switch). Also open: 15 (occupancy can be lost across a reset), 14 (sleep watchdogs around `System.sleep()`), 16, 17 (simplify series).
Accepted limitations, each with its ruling:
- **Durability exception (item 26):** at most one in-flight publish event can be lost per deliberate reset (library-level; fix is to write `curEvent` if `lib/` is touched). Out-of-memory, the two hardware watchdogs, the ApplicationWatchdog callback and HIBERNATE are not flushed, by design.
- **USB charge latch (item 22):** alert 22 may clear early after a real charge fault and can mask alert 20 (`docs/reference/alert-codes.md`).
- **Binding-delete guard:** bounded per reset cause, not per power-on (a hardware reset replaces the reset data); `resetCount` +1 on its reset; an armed chip watchdog could survive the unbind (covered by the v50 exercise).
- **LED-gate gap:** a *trusted* piece with the LED low is not closed on a PIR wake (as in v48); C2 fixes it for untrusted pieces only.
- **Toolchain note:** the CI binary is 168 bytes larger than the local build of the same source (accepted; the binary that ships is the one that was soaked).
- **Particle upload:** the title length limit and the action's behaviour on an existing version are unverified; the v49.1 console file is named `firmware-boron-6.6.0.bin` (fixed for the next release), content identical.

## 6. The release pipeline
- **Workflow** `.github/workflows/build.yml`: `host-tests` (the shell suite under zsh, Python tests), `firmware` (Particle's buildpack image on the runner, no Particle token; the tag's major × 100 + iteration must equal the compiler's view of `FIRMWARE_PRODUCT_VERSION`), `publish` (GitHub Release with the `.bin` and `.sha256`; annotated tag at the built commit; no checkout; the write token on one step), `upload` (Particle product firmware version, behind a GitHub Environment `particle-42131` approved by Chip per run). Third-party actions are pinned by full SHA.
- **Protections:** a branch ruleset (PR plus CI) and an immutable `v*` tag ruleset; `CODEOWNERS` on the workflows; no workflow can pin firmware, set a default or flash a device.
- **Token policy:** only product-scoped API users, one scope each (`firmware:create` for the upload), stored in the Environment secret, never in the repo or logs (`docs/notes/particle-api-users-register.md`). No dry-run upload exists (a test version could not be removed).

## 7. Questions for the reviewer
1. **Clock trust.** Trust is "a cloud sync this boot, under 24 h old"; any reset makes the clock untrusted (items 18, 19, 21). Is a persisted last-sync epoch withdrawn on evidence (a large correction, an invalid RTC, an oscillator-fault event) sound, or is there a failure mode the rule hides?
2. **Bounded loss in item 19.** We accept losing one partial session per reboot while untrusted instead of crediting a possibly merged span. Is that the right trade for people-counting data?
3. **Reset and persistence paths.** v49 flushes before seven deliberate-reset sites and excludes OOM, the two hardware watchdogs, the ApplicationWatchdog callback and HIBERNATE. What reset or power-loss path, and which store, might we have missed?
4. **The AB1805 switch plan.** Is replacing a vendored RTC/watchdog library with the built-in driver in one merged set, with v49 as the rollback floor and a binding guard, safe? What could leave two owners of the chip or an armed chip watchdog across a rollback?
5. **The recovery ladder.** Connectivity failsafe: stage 2 reset, stage 3 timed power-down, with no remote reset for a device in the field. Are the stages, cooldowns and "Unknown openness counts as open" assumptions sound?
6. **The pipeline's trust model.** One third-party action holds the Particle upload token (scope `firmware:create`, Environment-gated); the tag message is read through the API; tags are immutable but the moved-annotated-tag residual is accepted. Is that acceptable for a public repository?
7. **The process.** AI implementer, a different AI verifier that must quote the commit SHA, an AI architect, a human gate for merge/pin/default. Where would you expect this to fail silently (shared blind spots, verifier reading the implementer's report)?
8. **What would you cut?** The source has grown a lot of diagnostics, test-only hooks and recovery layers; which would you remove, and what is the smallest safe core?
