## Findings — one-page overview

**1. The logged 0/0 does not establish no signal.** Before network readiness, Device OS returns invalid −1 percentages; the app rounds them to zero and marks them valid (`wiring/src/spark_wiring_cellular.cpp:28`; `src/state/State_Connect.cpp:63`). Dev-09/14 have 122/30 captured failures, all about 660 seconds, but no ConnDiag time series. Neither Singapore nor US evidence establishes a safe zero-duration cutoff N. Device OS already resets the modem after its 600-second registration threshold; preserve periodic full recovery. Appendix A links every failure; §1 verifies the reset clock and original Dev-09 warning.

**2. Low-charge failures need never reach the deep budget.** Selection is configured 120–900 seconds, otherwise 300, overridden to 660 for SoC >50% or counter ≥3. Only success increments the counter (`src/state/State_Connect.cpp:105`, `:552`, `:663`). All 246 captured Singapore starts use 660; US events cannot identify the selected budget or overrides. Repair the existing 300/660 ladder to count failed attempts and provide periodic full recovery; remove blanket deep selection above 50%. Appendix A and §2 document observations and `git log -S` provenance.

**3. Slow teardown costs time; separate timeout loops also occur.** Dev-09/14 have 32/33 completed waits >7 seconds, totalling 541.588/487.182 seconds, plus 14/5 separate ~30-second timeouts (Appendix B, each linked). PPP error 5 means user interruption (`third_party/lwip/lwip/src/include/netif/ppp/ppp.h:136`). Standby suppression trades another attachment for lower sleep radio power (`src/state/State_Sleep.cpp:378`). No causal report loss or missed wake is demonstrated for completed waits. Keep ordinary teardown unchanged pending measurement; investigate repeated preparation timeouts separately. This evidence is Singapore-only.

**4. Thirty connectivity-stage breadcrumb-18 watchdog records are distinct from diagnostic backlog resets.** There are 22 US/eight Singapore cases, paired with reason-60 startup events; 80 diag publications represent 79 distinct payloads (Appendix C). PCKL1’s [1 October 03:01:59Z status](../../build-tmp/connectivity-archive/2026-10-01/status/e00fce6865fe7770b80a014e/2026-10-01T03-01-59-333Z.json) instead reports reason 140. Failsafe stage 2 writes breadcrumb 10 before reset (`src/Generalized-Core-Counter.cpp:2627`); it does not fit. ThrashGuard/cloud reset remain unproved candidates. MAFC-1/Court3 have no post-silence recovery events here.

**5. OTA can be interrupted by sleep; extend the existing update state.** Device OS clears pending at download start (`system/src/firmware_update.cpp:163`), so `updatesPending()` cannot protect an active transfer. Dev-09’s five visible v30 sessions end in five queue-gate failures before overnight hibernate; the closing report arrives 28,903.520 seconds late. Dev-14 completes the same image in 40.081 device seconds (Appendix D). US events establish rollout/reset histories, not session counts: MAFC-1’s queued v25 reset is **stage=sleep, bc=28**; Court3’s three diag resets remain separate. Estimated fix: **50–90 source lines**, using begin/progress/complete/failed, an inactivity timeout and sleep/ack integration.

**6. Failed acquisition dominates measured Singapore awake time.** Captured failure timers total **80,520.234 seconds (22.37 h), Dev-09**, and **19,800.040 seconds (5.50 h), Dev-14**, versus 1,028.770 seconds of combined slow completed teardown (Appendices A/B/E). Appendix E separates daily observed costs, nominal watchdog timing and unmeasured intervals. Fleet conclusions rest on US evidence and shared code, not extrapolated Singapore radio performance.

### 2. Budget selection, history and configurations

The selection order matters: valid configured 120–900 → fallback 300 → unconditional deep override 660 (`src/state/State_Connect.cpp:105`). Therefore a configured 900 can produce 900 only when SoC ≤50 and counter <3; the “deep” path actually *reduces* it to 660. Conversely configured 120–299 is allowed on a normal attempt; the header's comment requiring DEFAULT ≥300 is not an enforced minimum on configured values (`src/power/ConnectivityPolicy.h:50`, `:62`). The three preceding counter increments are successful connects, not necessarily three failures. Counter reset occurs at deep entry and success increments it again; failure never increments it (`src/state/State_Connect.cpp:281`, `:552`, `:663`).

There is one production attempt-budget evaluator. Recovery stage 1 at 60 seconds and stage 2 at 120 seconds do not choose another overall budget or restart the attempt timestamp (`src/state/State_Connect.cpp:235`, `:393`). Mode affects whether a connection is requested and sleep policy, not this evaluator. `activeConnectAttemptWithinBudget()` reads the selected budget for the long failsafe deferral (`:161`); the long failsafe is a separate supervisor, not a 900-second attempt budget (`src/Generalized-Core-Counter.cpp:2593`).

Configuration validation accepts **30–900** (`src/cloud/ConfigApply.cpp:304`). Actual connection override accepts **120–900**; 30–119 falls back to 300, potentially then 660. The idle ceiling independently accepts **30–900**, with fallback 300 (`src/state/State_Idle.cpp:305`). It applies only when its powered/no-work/not-healthy-connected conditions hold, not simply whenever connected (`:291`). The default stored value is 300 (`src/Config.h:35`, `src/MyPersistentData.cpp:154`). Those are three different constraints on a shared setting; the inconsistency should be resolved deliberately rather than globally replacing every “300.”

### 3. Teardown causality, costs and destinations

Appendix B contains every captured completed teardown >7 seconds and every 30-second timeout, with archive links, mode, standby flags and evidence for destination. The counts are **capture counts**, not rates: USB drops hide many normal and abnormal endings. Values >10 seconds trigger the app's health rule: 26 Dev-09 / 23 Dev-14 completed waits, 902.722 seconds combined; latency above a hypothetical five-second reference is 657.722 seconds. That reference is an accounting comparison, **not proven removable latency**. Health-warning lines are only 14/9 because logging occurs on reason changes; do not count warning lines as teardowns (`src/state/State_Sleep.cpp:101`).

The sequence is request cloud disconnect, request cellular disconnect, request off, then poll completion; standby uses cloud-only disconnect (`src/power/Connectivity.h:25`, `:38`; `src/state/State_Sleep.cpp:761`). Device OS tears PPP down and disconnects NCP (`hal/network/lwip/cellular/pppncpnetif.cpp:331`); Sara disconnect checks AT and sets minimum functionality (`hal/network/ncp_client/sara/sara_ncp_client.cpp:474`), then off attempts soft off/mux stop/hardware fallback (`:404`). `PPPERR_USER=5` is a user-requested close (`third_party/lwip/lwip/src/include/netif/ppp/ppp.h:136`); connection loss is 6. The captured sequence of PPP 5 immediately after app teardown supports **app-initiated teardown**, not network-initiated detach. Exact PDP context/network signalling duration is not logged, so active-PDP versus carrier delay cannot be separated. Many completed rows have cloud time only ~14–62 ms while modem time accounts for essentially all of the delay (Appendix B).

Standby suppression acts only where standby would otherwise be requested: IKA mode, open hours and a successful session with the radio still on. It removes INACTIVE_STANDBY from ULP and forces off; it does not change normal closed-hours hibernate, which already powers down (`src/state/State_Sleep.cpp:367`, `:378`, `:1334`). Lower sleep radio power is exchanged for another attach later; current in mA and resulting mAh are not measured here. Recovery needs a successful connection **and** teardown ≤5 seconds (`:127`), so repeated successful cold teardowns lasting >5 seconds can leave suppression latched for the boot. No matched trials establish whether this tradeoff pays back. The code's comment suggesting 30–60 seconds reconnection is not a measured fleet result and is not used as a cost estimate. Separately, `modemUnstable` defers automatic IKA/aligned reconnect requests until 30 seconds after teardown; service requests, occupancy changes, daily close and the forced webhook-health path bypass that defer (`src/state/State_Report.cpp:26`, `:191`, `:210`, `:231`). It is a request deferral, not 30 seconds proven radio-on.

The separate 19 timeouts total **570.039 seconds** of explicit wait. At expiry the app raises alert 15 and enters ERROR_STATE (`src/state/State_Sleep.cpp:804`). Low-power mode clears selected connectivity/sleep alerts, or an existing higher-severity watchdog alert can leave resolution=0; ERROR→IDLE→SLEEP can retry (`src/state/State_Error.cpp:128`, `:147`; `src/MyPersistentData.cpp:863`). Dev-14 visibly repeats this on 30 September beginning 03:30:35Z. This is a failed preparation/bounded timeout, not proof System.sleep failed or a wake was missed. No lost report can be causally assigned to the 7–30-second **completed** teardowns; a queue timeout before teardown is a separate event. Do not fix PPP error 5 by adding detach/reset calls.

### 4. Connection stalls, reset candidates and present bounds

Appendix C gives all 30 exact stage-filtered occurrences, associated startup status, queued events and connection age. Every one ends in a reported watchdog recovery, with paired status reason 60 where available. The 60-second watchdog timeout measures time since refresh, not the time since last successful connection. `connAge` is sampled millis since `connectedStartMs`; sleep and earlier awake work can be included, and a stalled code path can prevent the final elapsed-stage update (`src/Generalized-Core-Counter.cpp:630`). Thus `elapsed=0` does not mean a zero-duration stall and `connAge=5030585` does not mean 84 minutes continuously stuck. A watchdog publication may wait for later connectivity; apparent pre-event “success” in bench serial is often the **post-reset delivery connection**. It must not be labelled a cause. There is no reliable per-occurrence RF/teardown/recovery-stage attribution in this copy. ### 5. OTA mechanism and minimal repair

The saved [updatesPending page](https://docs.particle.io/reference/device-os/api/ota-updates/system-updatespending/) includes newer behavior: an online product can receive a pending notification even with updates enabled. The older disabled-updates example is not an exclusive description of 6.4.1. `systemEventHandler()` accepts `particle/device/updates/pending` and sets the pending flag without a disabled-updates predicate (`system/src/system_cloud_internal.cpp:118`). `System.updatesPending()` reads that flag (`wiring/inc/spark_wiring_system.h:828`); actual download start clears it and sets internal updating state (`system/src/firmware_update.cpp:163`). A later cloud notification can set pending again; **neither true nor false is a reliable active-download test**.

That is the code path explaining Dev-09's “Entering FIRMWARE_UPDATE_STATE”: the app entered because pending was true before/around start, not because it had a transfer event subscriber (`src/state/State_Connect.cpp:651`). At 12:35:47 receive time, the entry line has device millis **37,661,836**, whereas “Starting firmware update” received one second earlier has millis **37,661,840**. The app entry actually preceded the start by 4 ms; the later exit at millis 37,663,013 fits clearing pending at begin: [Dev-09 · 2026-09-30T12:35:47.482768+00:00](../../build-tmp/connectivity-archive/2026-09-30/serialLog/e00fce68399ee6244a963935/2026-09-30T12-35-47-482768+00-00.json); [Dev-09 · 2026-09-30T12:35:46.516968+00:00](../../build-tmp/connectivity-archive/2026-09-30/serialLog/e00fce68399ee6244a963935/2026-09-30T12-35-46-516968+00-00.json); [Dev-09 · 2026-09-30T12:35:52.603315+00:00](../../build-tmp/connectivity-archive/2026-09-30/serialLog/e00fce68399ee6244a963935/2026-09-30T12-35-52-603315+00-00.json). The exact inbound pending packet is not archived, so packet-level cause is inferred from the sole code path, not directly observed.

Use `firmware_update` begin/progress/complete/failed from the saved [system-events reference](https://docs.particle.io/reference/device-os/api/system-events/system-events-reference/) (`system/inc/system_event.h:52`, `:77`). A public source-verified alternative for reconciliation is **`System.updateStatus()` compared with `UpdateStatus::IN_PROGRESS`** (`wiring/inc/spark_wiring_system.h:126`, `:846`; `system/src/system_update.cpp:363`), which checks the real updating object before pending. Its source is verified locally; no separate documentation page is cited from memory.

A small integration should latch the events, perform state transitions on the application loop, and let **actual chunk progress** refresh update-state ThrashGuard progress. Simply entering on begin and leaving on complete/failed is **necessary but insufficient**: a download can begin after CONNECTING has returned to SLEEPING; the sleep gate currently has a timeout even if update is a blocker; pending=false currently exits the state; button/global report transitions and the five-minute absolute cap also remain escape paths (`src/state/State_Connect.cpp:652`, `:752`, `:760`, `:771`; `src/state/State_Sleep.cpp:476`; `src/Generalized-Core-Counter.cpp:1683`). Recheck active transfer before committing teardown/sleep, and route active OTA back to the existing state. Treat complete as completion/reboot-pending until Device OS performs its scheduled update reset, not an invitation to hibernate between completion and apply (`system/src/firmware_update.cpp:220`). These are integration changes to existing paths, not a new state machine.

**Cap:** keep five minutes for waiting for an update that has not begun. For a live transfer, replace absolute residence timeout with **five minutes without real transfer progress**, using the existing Device OS timeout/failed event as the primary exit (`communication/src/firmware_update.h:101`; `communication/src/firmware_update.cpp:177`). Do not reset that clock for a mere connection or pending notification. Align the update-specific ThrashGuard deadline with Device OS's 300-second inactivity cancellation, allowing a short failure-event handling margin; its present 180 seconds would otherwise pre-empt that policy. Refresh only on actual transfer progress, never on each loop or a pending notification (`src/ThrashGuard.cpp:66`). After failure/cancellation, return to normal bounded service/sleep, clear the active latch and re-arm ack timing. A missed-event fallback can reconcile `updateStatus()`; a 300-second healthy download should not be cut off simply because it is slow. The observed Dev-14 same-image transfer is **40.081 device seconds**, not its 27.425-second receive-time delta. Dev-09's same image advanced 0→16,384→23,040→39,936→64,512 bytes across five sessions; the last pre-hibernate attempt still fails the queue gate. This supports protecting progress, not choosing an arbitrarily larger fixed cap. Appendices D/E quantify captured sessions.

**Queue and ack:** Device OS `send_event` returns BUSY during OTA (`communication/inc/protocol.h:508`). Queue retry retains/re-files failed events (`lib/PublishQueuePosixRK/src/PublishQueuePosixRK.cpp:367`) and reset/disconnect writes queued events to files (`:402`). Thus keeping the transfer awake does not mean webhooks can drain concurrently. Current ack supervision begins at cloud success, before actual publish completion, and raises 40 after the configured interval (`src/state/State_Connect.cpp:544`; `src/Generalized-Core-Counter.cpp:1655`). Suspend that deadline for active OTA; after failure/reconnect, give the queued report a fresh publish/ack opportunity. Do not clear the queue or invent acknowledgements. Successful OTA reboots and later service drains the persisted queue. Dev-09 logs an attempted alert-40 raise five times during v30; payload alert remains 19 because severity arbitration preserves the stronger watchdog alert (`src/MyPersistentData.cpp:899`).

US evidence does not establish the same multi-session sleep interruption. Appendix D lists each rollout's payload-version transition and queued resets. In particular MAFC-1's watchdog published 10:01:37.564Z under metadata v28 belongs to a **v25 sleep-path reset** (bc28, queue200), as the adjacent startup payload explicitly says v25. Court3 received/published v25→v29→v30 history with a v25 sleep reset, a persisted failsafe-stage-2 reset, and three subsequent diagnostic-stage resets; one diagnostic event was retransmitted. These are real problems but neither the sleep-path reset nor the diagnostic backlog is evidence for stage=connectivity WO-2026-09-25-005. Event publish UTC is not necessarily update/reset occurrence UTC.
