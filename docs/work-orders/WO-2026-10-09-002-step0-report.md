# WO-2026-10-09-002 Step 0: the once-a-day config window

- **Base:** `5b4761e` on `wo/2026-10-09-002-config-window`. This is main `83df4a3` (v40) plus the dispatch doc, so `src/` is the same as `83df4a3`.
- **Date:** 2026-10-09
- **Model used:** claude-sonnet-5-5, reasoning: high (set by `--effort high`)

**Plain goal:** a ledger config change reaches every sleeping device within a day. The gate holds only on the first connection after open hour and on the boot connection. It holds briefly after the outbound syncs and ends early on an input `onSync`. No other connection pays anything.

**Verdict: PROCEED.** The estimate is about +17 `src/` lines, in `LedgerClient.cpp` plus one line in `State_Sleep.cpp`. The proposal uses RAM-only statics and needs no persisted field, timer or schedule.

## 1. Reuse of the per-connection window

**Today, `Cloud::areLedgersSynced()` (`LedgerClient.cpp`):**

| Situation | What it does | Cite |
|---|---|---|
| Not connected | Sets `wasDisconnected`, clears the observed epoch, returns false | `:229-235` |
| Connected, new connection | Detects it from `lastConnection` changing (or `wasDisconnected`) and restarts the window at the first call | `:113-134` |
| Warm: both `lastSynced()` > 0 | Returns true at once, however little time has passed | `:138-152` |
| Window open (≤10 s on cellular) | Returns false | `:228` |
| After the window: default synced, device empty | True | `:162-177` |
| After the window: device synced, default empty | True | `:179-194` |
| After the window: only one synced and the other has content | False, so alert 44 at the budget | `:196-212` |
| After the window: neither synced | True | `:213-226` |

- **INF, what "cold" and "warm" mean:** `lastSynced` is loaded from flash at init (`ledger.cpp:561` in Device OS 6.4.1). So "cold" (0/0) means a ledger never synced. Nearly every connection after a device's first sync is warm, including boots. The 7 Oct OBS does not say whether `lastSynced` was 0 at its three cold releases.
- **OBS, the window's anchor:** it starts at the first helper call after `lastConnection` changes. That first call comes from the gate (`State_Sleep.cpp:510`). `State_Connect.cpp:560` calls the helper only on a config failure. So the window runs from gate entry, not from the cloud connect.
- **Skipping the early return:**
  - **OBS:** on a warm connection it falls to `:156` and holds until 10 s after the window anchor. Then `:197-211` finds `bothSynced` and returns true.
  - **OBS:** an inbound completion does advance `lastSynced` (`ledger_manager.cpp:1307`, just before `notifySynced` at `:1318`). But nothing in the window path looks at it.
  - **INF:** skipping alone gives a fixed hold, not "end on `onSync`". The early end needs a new comparison.
- **Signals that exist but don't work:** `ledgersSynced` is set true in the callbacks (`LedgerClient.cpp:54,66`) and never reset (`Cloud.cpp:199`). `pendingConfigApply` is consumed by `Cloud::loop` every pass (`Generalized-Core-Counter.cpp:1665`, `Cloud.cpp:678-679`). Neither is a per-connection signal.
- **`lastSynced` as the early-end signal:** a snapshot of `max(default, device)` `lastSynced()` taken at the new-connection edge works without a callback. Comparing with `!=` also survives a clock step.
- **Alert 44, `4c2b734` (v8, 2026-04-23):**
  - **OBS, the message:** "Release v8 with ledger sync window fix".
  - **OBS, the `CHANGELOG.md` 8.00 "Fixed" entries:**
    - "Alert 44 false positives after reconnect: now returns true immediately when both ledgers are already synced instead of forcing the full connection window."
    - "Stale ledger sync window reuse across cycles: resets its timing window on each newly observed cloud connection."
  - **OBS, the diff in `LedgerClient.cpp`:**
    - It adds `lastObservedConnectionEpoch` and the `newConnectionObserved` reset.
    - It adds the `defaultSynced && deviceSynced` early return.
    - The partial-sync branch is unchanged.
  - **OBS, the original failure:** before `4c2b734` the window was set only when `wasDisconnected` was true. Both call sites (`State_Sleep.cpp:124`, `State_Connect.cpp:209` at `4c2b734^`) ran only while connected. So the helper never saw a disconnect, and the window was set once per boot and went stale.
  - **INF, the failure's mechanism:** the changelog does not spell out the path to the alert. The likely path is that a stale window plus a one-ledger-synced state keeps the helper at false until the 30 s gate budget, which raises alert 44. I could not confirm that from the diff.
  - **INF, why the proposal is safe:**
    - The proposal restores neither failure. The early return stays on every connection except the one where the hold is active.
    - The hold resets on the same per-connection edge, so no stale state carries over.
    - It is bounded by the window and by an end condition.
    - The hold is "wait out the window", which is the "forced full window" that `4c2b734` removed. That is why it must be limited to the daily and boot connections.
- **Gate behaviour during the hold:**
  - **OBS:** `ledgersSynced = config && output` (`State_Sleep.cpp:516`), so the ledger blocker stays set and `GateRelease reason=ledger` is logged.
  - **OBS:** the hold cannot be told apart from an output wait in the label.
  - **OBS:** the budget is 30 s base. It ratchets to 70 s if the output ledger was ever pending (`:527-530`), and it is checked at `:598`.
  - **INF:** a 10 s hold fits inside 30 s. The only overrun is when the output ledger clears within 10 s of the 70 s end, which gives alert 44 (`:641`) once on that day.

## 2. "Once a day" with no new persisted field

| Marker | Boot | First open-hour connection | Never connects in the first hour | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `DailyBoundary::check` | No | No | n/a | It tests the **close** (`DailyBoundary.cpp:34-36`). It is consumed by `lastDailyCleanup` (`State_Report.cpp:82`) and drives the close connection (`State_Report.cpp:229`). It is the wrong end of the day. |
| `DailyBoundary::todayAt(get_openTime())` | n/a | Yes, as the reference time | Yes | The failsafe already uses it (`Generalized-Core-Counter.cpp:2617-2618`). |
| `lastConnection` | Persisted, so a same-day reboot looks "already connected" | Yes, but see below | Yes | It is overwritten at `State_Connect.cpp:513`, before the gate runs. At the gate it holds the current connection, not the previous one. |
| `session.firstConnectionObserved` | Yes (RAM) | No | No | It is set at `State_Connect.cpp:604`, before the gate, and the gate has no edge to key on. |
| RAM static in the helper | Yes (0 after boot) | Yes | Yes | Chosen. |

- **Chosen:** a RAM static `holdDoneEpoch` (the connection whose hold finished), tested at the new-connection edge: `holdDoneEpoch < DailyBoundary::todayAt(openTime)`.
- **Coverage, INF:**
  - After a boot `holdDoneEpoch` is 0, so the boot connection holds.
  - The first connection at any time after today's open holds, whether it is the first hour or later.
  - A hold that is cut short (teardown, occupancy exit, `GateFail`) does not set `holdDoneEpoch`, so the next connection holds again.
- **Edge:** a connection before open (a 03:00 boot, say) sets `holdDoneEpoch` below today's open, so it can hold twice that day. That is bounded and rare.
- **Untrusted clock:** guard the test with `Clock::isTrusted()`, as `DailyBoundary::check` does (`:34`). Without it a 1970 clock would hold on every connection. With it, the device skips the hold, which is today's behaviour.
- **Per-boot reset:** accepted. A boot connection is wanted anyway, and no persisted state is needed.
- **Mode:**
  - INTERMITTENT and KEEP_ALIVE both run the sleep gate each wake. KEEP_ALIVE still drops the cloud session (the field case had five connections), so the same mechanism applies to both.
  - CONNECTED stays connected in open hours (`State_Sleep.cpp:406-409`) and receives pushes.
  - DISCONNECTED does not connect.
  - **Answer:** the mechanism should not differ by mode.

## 3. The hold's bound

| OBS | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Input `onSync` after the last outbound callback, 7 Oct | about 764 ms | WO-005 context |
| Input `onSync` after the STATUS callback, 9 Oct boot | about 1.08 s (14:26:59 to 14:27:00.081) | dispatch |
| Fetch still in flight after `GateRelease` | at least 3 s (14:00:41 to :44, 14:10:46 to :50) | dispatch |
| Three cold `-1001` cases, 7 Oct | within 20 ms of release (blockers 2.7, 7.5 and 3.3 s) | WO-005 evidence |

- **Proposal:** hold until an input `onSync` or `LEDGER_SYNC_TIMEOUT_MS` (10 s cellular, 5 s WiFi; `ConnectivityPolicy.h:170`), counted from the moment the output ledgers clear. No new constant.
- **Justification, INF:**
  - It is 9 to 13 times the observed landing latency.
  - It is more than twice the failure window of about 4 s.
  - It matches the repo's own "7 to 10 s on cellular" rationale.
- **Why count from the outbound clear:** Device OS orders outbound before inbound (`ledger_manager.cpp:497-533`). A window anchored at gate entry leaves only 2.5 s after a 7.5 s outbound wait, and nothing after 10 s. One line re-anchors `firstConnectedTime` while `hasPendingOutputLedgerSync()` is true.
- **No change pending:** the device pays the full N. The OS gives no way to tell "nothing to fetch" from "fetch not yet started". The cost is up to 10 s of extra connected time once per device-day, plus once per boot.

## 6. Stop conditions

**PROCEED.** The estimate is +17 against the +20 cap. The fix needs no persisted field, timer or schedule, and uses RAM-only statics.

## Open points

- **Two inbound ledgers:** ending the hold on the first advance can cut off a second ledger that is still in flight. It would land a day later. This is rare, because the usual change is `device-settings` alone. A tweak would be to extend by about 1 s after the first advance.
- **Alert 44 edge:** the output ledger clearing within 10 s of the 70 s budget would raise a false alert 44 once. It is minor and auto-cleared, and the hold is then marked done.
- **Not verified:** how long the Dev-09 boot connection stayed up before the gate. The `connectionMode: 0` case landed there, but I did not trace why.
- **Existing issue, not touched:** `GateRelease reason=ledger` cannot say whether the wait was input or output.
